## What blown-in insulation does

{config.displayName} blows loose-fill insulation into attics for homes and commercial properties across {config.primaryCity}, {config.secondaryCity} and {config.homeCounty} County. It goes in through a hose and settles over and around the framing, the ducts and the wiring, which is how it reaches the awkward corners that a cut batt never fits.

## Can you add it on top of what I already have?

Usually, yes. Topping up an attic that has gone thin or settled is one of the most common reasons to blow insulation in. Whether yours is worth adding to, or worth taking out first, is what the free inspection answers.

## Cellulose or fiberglass?

Both are used as loose fill. Cellulose is typically about R-3.2 to R-3.8 per inch and fiberglass about R-2.2 to R-2.7 per inch, so cellulose reaches a given R-value in less depth. Which suits your attic depends on the space, and we decide it when we look.

## How do I know if I have enough?

If the tops of the ceiling joists are showing, there is usually not much up there. Rather than guess, we measure what is in the attic during the free inspection and tell you where you stand.

## Blown-in or foam?

Blown-in is generally the lower cost route to a higher R-value. Foam costs more and also seals the air leaks. We price both against your own attic so you can see the difference rather than argue it in the abstract.
